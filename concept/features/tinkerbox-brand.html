Rank 8 · Brand concept

One friendly box. Nothing else competing for attention.

Explore “Tinkerbox” as a possible public name with an icon that keeps Tiny’s warmth while replacing the cloud metaphor with a small, useful container.

The box is the idea. The eyes make it ours.

Reason and value

Make the name and symbol tell the same story

Tinkerbox better describes a place where small apps are assembled from a fixed set of useful capabilities. A simple box character can carry that idea without changing the product promise: low ceremony, understandable constraints, and a friendly path to a secure result.

The PRD already treats TinyHost as a replaceable working label. This exploration changes no trust boundary, runtime, SDK behavior, or V1 commitment.

Three restrained concepts

Start with silhouette, not decoration

Recommended baseline

1. Friendly Box

A front-facing, softly rounded purple box with the existing cream eyes and tiny smile. A cobalt layer peeks from the lower-right, preserving the current offset treatment.

Strength: clearest and most recognizable at small sizes.

Risk: can feel generic unless the proportions, eye placement, and offset become distinctive.

Dimensional

2. Soft Parcel

A compact three-quarter package with a purple front, cobalt side, and cream top plane. The face stays on the front panel; corners are very round and toy-like rather than realistic.

Strength: communicates “box” immediately and gives Tinkerbox a stronger new silhouette.

Risk: perspective and extra faces can become noisy or resemble shipping software.

Playful

3. Peek-Open Box

A simple front-facing box whose lid sits slightly ajar. The eyes look out from the dark opening, with the cobalt lid acting as the only secondary shape.

Strength: feels curious and “ready to tinker” without adding loose confetti.

Risk: the face may become less legible at favicon size if the opening is too narrow.

Controlled vector study

Only the lid angle changes

One rounded three-dimensional box, two eyes inside, and one continuous lid surface. The body, perspective, colors, and eye placement stay fixed so the opening can be judged cleanly.

New direction · The working box

Let the box do the tinkering

Hands and tools communicate the name more directly than a moving lid. The box can stay nearly front-facing and geometrically simple, while two small gestures above its opening create the personality.

Most professional

1. Working Pair

Two abstract cream hands emerge from a dark open top. One holds a short screwdriver and one holds a tiny wrench. The box has no face, lid, fingers, or visible arms.

Why it works: the hands provide life while the tool silhouettes explain “tinker” immediately.

Recommended balance

2. Curious Helper

The familiar two eyes remain inside the box. One tool-holding hand rises from one side; a second hand rests on the opposite rim. There is only one tool, preferably a screwdriver.

Why it works: keeps the existing character while avoiding the visual noise of two tools plus a full face.

Most playful

3. Ready to Build

Two eyes and two small hands peek out together, with a wrench and screwdriver opening into a shallow V. The tools become the outer silhouette instead of decorative objects inside it.

Why it works: unmistakably lively and thematic, but only suitable as the larger character mark.

Visual rules

Minimal hands, recognizable tools

  • Remove the lid entirely; show the opening as one dark, rounded band.
  • Build each hand from one cream capsule with a single thumb notch—no fingers, cuffs, outlines, or cartoon gloves.
  • Use no more than two tools and reduce each to its most recognizable silhouette.
  • Keep the box purple with one cobalt side plane; tools may reuse cobalt with one small warm accent.
  • Use the plain box as the 16–32 px core mark and introduce hands and tools at 48–64 px and above.

Scope

  • Explore exactly three box silhouettes before choosing one.
  • Retain grape purple, cobalt blue, warm cream, and the friendly face.
  • Design first for 16–32 px recognition, then larger brand uses.
  • Produce raw, transparent, and cream-background variants after selection.
  • Keep colorful dots and shapes as an optional surrounding brand system.

Non-goals

  • No product, package, binary, SDK, hostname, or documentation rename yet.
  • No tools, gears, code brackets, clouds, or multiple floating objects inside the core icon.
  • No detailed cardboard folds, tape, labels, or realistic shipping cues.
  • No mascot body, hands, feet, or elaborate expression system.

Implementation sketch

Choose in monochrome, then add one secondary plane

  1. Sketch the three silhouettes in one color with the same face.
  2. Test each at 16, 24, 32, 64, and 512 pixels.
  3. Select the most ownable silhouette before applying cobalt depth.
  4. Refine one icon as clean vector geometry with transparent and cream variants.
  5. Only after the icon is approved, test the optional colorful flourish around it.

Open questions

  • Should the smile remain, or are the two eyes enough to carry the brand?
  • Should the box feel front-facing and flat, or visibly three-dimensional?
  • Should the hands replace the face, support the eyes, or become a larger-size character variant only?
  • Does “Tinkerbox” remain the product name, or become only the friendly public brand over existing technical identifiers?
  • Does the dimensional parcel read as creation and hosting, or too strongly as shipping?

Do or don’t

Do the exploration; do not rename the system yet

Do: The concept is cheap to test, the working name is explicitly replaceable, and a simpler symbol strengthens principle 14: keep the product understandable and constrained.

Don’t: Do not propagate Tinkerbox into binaries, packages, contracts, or public documentation until the name and icon survive visual, legal, and user validation.

Concept only; select one silhouette before producing new artwork.
